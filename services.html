Services

Work that begins with the question

Before the dataset, there is a question. Before the question, there is a decision someone needs to make. The work is designed around that sequence.

01

Data Analysis & Visualization

Analysis · Visualization · Reporting

The right analysis starts with the right question. Before the data is touched, it is worth understanding what decision needs to be made, what the data can and cannot show, and what a useful answer would actually look like.

From there, the work can involve exploratory analysis to understand what the data contains, more targeted analysis to answer a specific question, and visualization to communicate findings to an audience that may not spend their days in spreadsheets.

This can include statistical analysis, time-series analysis, comparative analysis across groups or geographies, and machine-learning approaches where they serve a genuine analytical purpose. The emphasis is on methods that are appropriate to the question, transparent in their assumptions, and communicable to the people who will use the results.

The principal works in Python, R, and SQL, and produces outputs in a range of formats — interactive web dashboards, static charts for reports, reproducible analytical notebooks, or narrative documents that walk through a finding and what it means.

Example deliverables

  • Exploratory analysis reports
  • Interactive dashboards
  • Data visualizations and charts
  • Reproducible analytical workflows
  • Statistical analyses and summaries
  • Data documentation and methodology notes
02

Research & Evidence Synthesis

Research · Synthesis · Policy Analysis

Many questions do not require new data. They require a careful look at what is already known.

Research and evidence synthesis involves locating relevant studies, datasets, and analyses; evaluating what they actually show; and assembling a coherent picture of the current state of evidence on a question. The emphasis is on honesty about what is established, what is contested, and where the evidence is thin.

This is useful when an organization needs to understand a domain before commissioning new research; when findings need to be translated for a non-specialist audience; when a funding proposal requires a literature grounding; or when a policy question requires an honest account of what the evidence supports and where it runs out.

The principal's background in international affairs, environmental policy, and natural resource management informs this work. Questions about climate adaptation, public investment, community resilience, housing, and sustainable development are familiar territory.

Example deliverables

  • Research briefs
  • Literature reviews
  • Evidence summaries
  • Policy analyses
  • Findings memos
  • Annotated bibliographies
03

Geospatial & Place-Based Analysis

Mapping · Spatial Analysis · GIS

Some questions are fundamentally about place: where things are happening, why they concentrate in certain areas, which communities are most affected, and what the geography reveals about the underlying patterns.

Geospatial analysis uses spatial data, mapping tools, and geographic methods to investigate these questions. The work ranges from producing maps for communication to conducting spatial analysis to identify patterns, clustering, or gaps — and building interactive mapping tools that let a non-technical audience explore the evidence directly.

This work often draws on public geographic data: Census boundaries and demographic datasets, FEMA flood and disaster datasets, land use and environmental records, satellite-derived data, and OpenStreetMap features. The principal works with Python (GeoPandas, Shapely), Leaflet, and standard GIS workflows, and has experience integrating spatial data with statistical analysis and interactive visualization.

Place-based analysis is also useful when narrative and context matter as much as the numbers — when the goal is to help an audience understand not only what the data shows, but where it shows it and why that geography matters.

Example deliverables

  • Interactive maps and mapping tools
  • Spatial analysis reports
  • Geospatial data processing and integration
  • Place-based research briefs
  • Map products for reports and presentations
  • Geographic data documentation
Engagements

How projects work

Project-based pricing

Glorious Morning Consulting works on a project basis. There are no posted hourly rates, standard packages, or guarantees about what the data will show.

An engagement begins with a conversation about the question, the available data, the intended use of the findings, and the scope of the work. From there, the principal will propose a set of deliverables, a timeline, and a fee.

This approach is intended to ensure that the work is scoped to what is actually needed — no more, and no less.
